# Purpose

> Why does Keystone exist?

Keystone exists to transform DataWiser's engineering knowledge into a reusable, standardized, and continuously evolving platform.

Rather than building software for the sake of software, Keystone captures proven engineering practices, operational experience, and automation into reusable capabilities.

These capabilities empower DataWiser consultants to deliver faster, more consistent, and higher-quality services while continuously improving through real customer engagements.

Keystone is not intended to replace engineering expertise.

Its purpose is to amplify engineering expertise.

---
# Core Philosophy

> What principles drive Keystone?

Keystone is built on engineering principles rather than specific technologies.

Database technologies evolve continuously. New platforms emerge, architectures change, and operational requirements shift over time.

Instead of building tools tightly coupled to a single database engine, Keystone focuses on reusable engineering capabilities that can be adapted across multiple data platforms.

Every capability within Keystone is designed to be:

- Knowledge-driven
- Technology-adaptive
- Repeatable
- Automated whenever possible
- Validated through real customer engagements

Keystone follows a consulting-first philosophy.

Capabilities are created to solve real engineering problems encountered by DataWiser consultants. As these capabilities mature through customer engagements, they evolve into reusable platform components.

Technology is the implementation detail.

Engineering is the product.

# Target Users

> Who is Keystone built for?

Keystone is designed with a layered user strategy.

### Primary Users

**DataWiser Engineers**

The primary users of Keystone are DataWiser engineers.

They use Keystone to perform assessments, execute operational tasks, automate repetitive work, and continuously improve engineering capabilities through real customer engagements.

Initially, this user group consists of the founder. As DataWiser grows, every engineer joining the company becomes both a user and a contributor to Keystone.

### Secondary Users

**Enterprise Customers**

Enterprise customers primarily consume the outputs generated by Keystone rather than interacting directly with the platform.

Typical outputs include:
- Health Assessment Reports
- Upgrade Assessments
- Architecture Reviews
- Operational Recommendations
- Capacity Reports

### Future Users

Organizations that do not engage DataWiser consulting services may use Keystone directly as a commercial platform.

In this scenario, Keystone serves database administrators, database reliability engineers, and engineering teams who wish to execute assessments and automation independently.

# Usage Scenarios

> How is Keystone used in practice? (Added 2026-10-05)

Keystone supports two primary usage scenarios. Both use the same evidence, Dictionary, rules and findings; they differ only in how evidence is acquired and how results are delivered.

### Continuous Assessment — long-term DBA engagements

DataWiser provides long-term DBA consulting to customers. In these engagements Keystone is installed in or near the customer environment and continuously collects evidence, evaluates it and maintains the engineering state of the systems.

Keystone must be able to operate **unattended**: it must keep collecting and assessing when the engineer is not actively using it, and it must notify the engineer when its engineering conclusions change (see ADR-0013). Nobody should have to run assessments repeatedly to discover that something needs attention.

Its value over time comes from history: recurring patterns, behaviour changes and outliers that a single snapshot cannot show.

This is the core usage scenario and corresponds to the Continuous Engineering operating mode.

### Manual Assessment — prospective customers and one-off health checks

A prospective customer asks for a health check. The engineer installs Keystone, collects evidence either once (snapshot) or over a short observation window such as one or two days, and delivers a report.

"Manual" means the assessment is started by the engineer rather than by a scheduler. It corresponds to the Snapshot Assessment and Temporary Observation operating modes.

### Positioning against one-off health check tools

Free one-off tools already exist. For example, `awslabs/pg-collector` is a single SQL script that produces a point-in-time HTML report with automated observations across roughly 19 categories, without history, trends or alerts.

- For Manual Assessment, such tools define the **minimum coverage bar**: a Keystone report should not cover less.
- Keystone's differentiation is continuity and engineering meaning: history, recurring patterns, outliers, a Finding lifecycle and finding-driven notification. Even a one- or two-day observation can reveal behaviour a single snapshot cannot.

### Independence from the founder

Keystone is first used by DataWiser in its own engagements. It must nevertheless be built to work without the founder present: installable from documentation, operable without hand-written SQL, and producing outputs that a customer or another engineer can understand. This is the prerequisite for later commercialisation.

# Customer Problems

> Which real-world problems does Keystone solve?

DataWiser repeatedly encounters the same engineering challenges across different customers, database engines, and environments.

These problems are often solved manually despite following similar engineering processes.

Keystone exists to standardize these recurring activities.

Typical customer problems include:
- Unknown database health status
- Upgrade planning and risk assessment
- Configuration inconsistencies
- Performance degradation
- Capacity planning
- Backup verification
- High Availability validation
- Disaster Recovery readiness
- Security and compliance reviews
- Operational documentation gaps

The objective is not merely to automate technical tasks, but to standardize engineering knowledge and deliver consistent outcomes.

# Business Model

> How does Keystone create value?

Keystone is developed first as an internal engineering platform supporting DataWiser consulting and managed database services.

Its initial commercial value comes from improving the quality, speed, consistency, and repeatability of consulting delivery rather than requiring direct software licensing revenue.

As capabilities mature through real engagements, selected parts of Keystone may later be offered as recurring services or a standalone commercial product.

The intended progression is:

**Consulting → Keystone-assisted services → repeatable managed capabilities → productized platform offerings**

# Product Scope

## In Scope
- High-level performance indicators as part of Health Assessment
- Detailed problem-driven Performance Investigation as a separate engineering service
- Root cause analysis across database, infrastructure, and application interaction where relevant
- Optional remediation after root cause identification

## Out of Scope

At its initial stages, Keystone focuses exclusively on database engineering.

The platform intentionally excludes areas outside DataWiser's current expertise, including but not limited to:
- General system administration
- Enterprise storage management
- Network engineering
- Virtualization platforms
- General DevOps tooling
- Cloud infrastructure provisioning unrelated to database workloads

These areas may be revisited in the future as DataWiser expands its engineering capabilities.

Current focus is more important than future possibilities.

Keystone also does not aim to:
- Guarantee that every performance problem originates in the database layer
- Automatically apply changes outside DataWiser's responsibility or authorization
- Treat Health Assessment as a substitute for detailed performance investigation
- Reproduce every capability of a general-purpose monitoring platform

# Commercial Philosophy

Keystone is not designed to generate engineering reports only.

Its purpose is to transform engineering findings into actionable implementation plans.

Every finding should support engineering prioritization, effort estimation, and customer proposal generation.

Commercial pricing remains a DataWiser business decision.

Keystone estimates engineering effort, while DataWiser determines commercial pricing based on its business model.

---
# Success Metrics

> How do we measure success?

Keystone is successful when it measurably improves engineering delivery.

Early indicators include:
- Less repeated manual analysis across similar engagements
- Faster and more consistent assessment delivery
- Reusable collectors and engineering rules validated against real environments
- Clear traceability from collected evidence to findings and recommendations
- Increasing use of Keystone capabilities in DataWiser customer work
- Reduced dependency on undocumented individual knowledge

Direct product revenue is a later-stage success metric, not the primary measure during the foundation phase.

# Future Vision

> Where should Keystone be in five years?

Keystone should become DataWiser's database engineering operating platform: a reusable foundation for assessments, continuous health analysis, performance engineering, operational automation, and controlled remediation across multiple database providers.

The long-term goal is not a collection of scripts. It is a system that preserves engineering knowledge, applies it consistently, and turns database evidence into explainable engineering decisions.

Commercial productization remains a possible outcome of that maturity rather than a requirement imposed on the early architecture.
